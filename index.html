'LIGHTNING': swerve; counter-rotating roller coral intake; riveted sheet-metal arm; 2D AprilTag auto-align

Canada · 68-7 · #1 · High confidence
Robot: LIGHTNING
Scoring (coral / algae)
Coral and algae end effector unconfirmed (see the drawing package). The team's posts describe a 'dunk' scoring step that the driver triggers after auto-align. The driver keeps this step so the robot can rescore after a miss and lose a second or two, not a full cycle.
Intake
Roller intake with top rollers and a bottom roller that turn in opposite directions; a small 1:1 gearbox reverses the bottom roller. The front roller floats, and coral compression changes with coral orientation. WCP stretch star wheels sit on 1.5 in OD, 1/8 in wall polycarbonate tube. Changed after practice from 1.25 in OD, 1/16 in wall tube after the tube bent in practice and the wheels migrated and slipped. The polycarbonate pivot is clamped with aluminum plates that spread bolt loads in impacts.
Elevator / arm
The arm is a riveted and formed 1/16 in aluminum structure. The robot uses 5052 aluminum sheet at 1/16 in and 1/8 in depending on load. The team states it drives pivots with large aluminum sprockets on a dead axle and chain: #25 for light-duty and #35 for heavy-duty pivots. Elevator details unconfirmed (see the drawing package).
Drivetrain
Swerve. The team states its swerve control and odometry are fully custom, not CTRE's generated swerve code. They use Phoenix Pro features: FOC, fused CANcoder and time synchronization. Module type unconfirmed (see the drawing package).
Endgame
Unconfirmed (not stated in the public release).
Auto
Waypoints are hard-coded points, with no path-planning interface. The tolerance per waypoint ranges from 1.5 in to 48 in, depending on the action. In auto, the robot computes the coral position and generates a drive point with a heading that points at the coral. Auto selection inputs are read only after a 3 s timer in disabled, so the driver cannot change them between auto and teleop. Coral count per routine unconfirmed.
Vision
Limelight with 2D AprilTag tracking only; the team does not use MegaTag 1 or MegaTag 2. Distance comes from an interpolating tree map of tag area to measured distance. Auto-align drives to a pose relative to the reef tag for L2-L4 and uses global pose for L1. A drive-to-point controller runs two PID loops, on translation and rotation error, with an aggressive slew rate limiter.
Why study it
PICK (binder): rank 1 of 3690 on Local EPA, Daly Division winner and FIRST Ontario Provincial Championship winner. The team released a technical binder and a drawing package for 'LIGHTNING', not a CAD model. Study the auto-align. The robot uses only 2D Limelight tag data, with no MegaTag pose. It drives to a pose a set distance and heading from the reef tag for L2-L4, with distance from a tag-area map. A two-PID drive-to-point controller with a slew rate limiter moves the robot, and the driver triggers the final dunk so a miss can be rescored. The drawing package keeps every part revision, marked R1, R2 and so on. Team replies in the release thread add the intake roller tube change made after a practice failure.
Lessons for students
  • Drawings can document a robot without a CAD model: the drawing package keeps every part revision, marked R1, R2 and so on, so a student can follow the changes through the season.
  • Auto-align without a field pose: the robot measures the reef tag relative to itself and drives to a pose a set distance and heading from it, using global pose only for L1.
  • A material fix after a practice failure: the star-wheel roller tube went from 1.25 in OD, 1/16 in wall to 1.5 in OD, 1/8 in wall polycarbonate after it bent and the wheels slipped.
  • Aluminum plates on a polycarbonate pivot act as large washers and spread bolt loads, so side impacts do not crack the polycarbonate at the bolt holes.
  • The team prefers chain to 5M HTD belt on pivots because chain is less likely to skip under shock loads, and designs in a chain tensioner, such as the inline turnbuckle tensioner used on the intake.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. A full technical binder PDF and a drawing package PDF that keeps part revisions. Team replies in the release thread add detail on auto-align, the swerve software, the intake rollers and sheet-metal materials. Released 2025-05-29 on Chief Delphi.

★ Jack in the Bot
'Spectre': shoulder, extension and wrist arm; latch climber with automated climb; reef-AprilTag auto-align; swerve
113
Local EPA
Yes
USA-WA · 66-7 · #3 · High confidence
Robot: Poi Pounder XXVI Spectre
Scoring (coral / algae)
End effector on the arm wrist. It holds coral straight, as the intake delivers it, or horizontal in an L1-optimized position, and it also handles algae. Scoring uses one button for a 'base' setpoint and one for a 'top' setpoint. Translation, rotation, release, wait and backout are all automated. Rollers and motors: Unconfirmed (see CAD and tech binder).
Intake
Intake type: Unconfirmed (see CAD and tech binder). The team states it never used coral station intaking during teleop. On station loading, a team reply says it is better to drive firmly into the station and guarantee the load than to risk wheel slip leaving the robot short.
Elevator / arm
Arm with a shoulder pivot, an extension and a wrist. The code treats each as its own subsystem, which the team says gives cleaner compartmentalization. Shoulder and wrist are commanded by angle. Positions come from the built-in Kraken encoders on the shoulder, extension and wrist. A re-homing button was used at most twice in the season, after heavy arm impacts; it mainly saves time in driver practice. The arm code ran on the team's 2023 robot before bring-up. Motor count unconfirmed (see CAD).
Drivetrain
Swerve; the team describes hard-resetting the swerve odometry from vision. Module type unconfirmed (see CAD). A skew compensation constant corrects drift off center when the robot rotates while translating. The code uses voltage targets, not duty cycle, so output holds as battery voltage drops; the reply does not say which motors.
Endgame
Climber with latches and climber intake wheels. The climb is fully automated once the bar is in the latches. The code detects the bar from the speed and current of the climber intake wheel motor, and the team reports no false positives.
Auto
Autonomous Award at PNW District Auburn, Sammamish and the PNW DCMP. The team mentions a 'mark auto' and tested code to knock algae off the mark; that code was not used. Routines and piece counts unconfirmed (see code).
Vision
Cameras read reef AprilTags. There is no pose estimator: the code hard-resets swerve odometry to camera poses, and only while auto-aligning, when the robot is stable. The team reports vision as accurate at the scoring position, with more error farther out. Drivers switch between pose-based and rotation-based reef selection; LEDs show purple for pose-based and yellow for rotation-based.
Why study it
PICK (CAD + code): 'Spectre', 2025 Championship Winner and rank 3 on Local EPA, with Onshape CAD, a tech binder and the 2025 robot code. Study the driver automation the team explains in its threads. Auto-align is a PID controller with a capped velocity and friction compensation; the team tried trapezoidal profiles and dynamic path following and kept the simpler controller. Vision feeds no pose estimator: odometry is hard-reset to reef-AprilTag poses only while auto-aligning. Scoring is one button per setpoint, with translation, rotation, release and backout automated. Each button changes action with the piece held: straight coral, horizontal coral, algae or none. The climb runs itself once the bar is in the latches, sensed from intake-wheel speed and current.
Lessons for students
  • A plain PID auto-align with a velocity cap and friction compensation can be enough: the team tried trapezoidal profiles and dynamic path following and kept PID.
  • Relocalize only when the data is good: odometry resets from vision happen during auto-align, not during fast traversals.
  • Give drivers a fallback when odometry drifts: rotation-based reef selection starts auto-align without trusting pose, and LEDs show the mode.
  • Motor data can replace a sensor: the climber detects the bar in the latches from intake-wheel speed and current.
  • Bind buttons to robot state: one button does four things depending on the piece held (straight coral, horizontal coral, algae, none). Drivers use Xbox controllers with back paddles. The arm code was proven on the 2023 robot before bring-up.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Public Onshape CAD, a tech binder (Google Drive) and the 2025 robot code on GitHub. Team replies in the reveal and code threads explain the auto-align control, vision relocalization, automated scoring, controller bindings and arm sensing. Released 2025-04-23 on Chief Delphi.

Robot: SubLime
Scoring (coral / algae)
The team's 2025 SystemCore thread lists separate beam breaks for a coral end effector and an algae end effector. All scoring runs on one controller through back paddles, with an autoscore function. End effector rollers, motors and geometry: Unconfirmed (see CAD).
Intake
The SystemCore thread lists an indexer beam break. Coral station or ground intake and algae intake: Unconfirmed (see CAD).
Elevator / arm
Sideways elevator driven by Dyneema cable at a 3:1 reduction; the OP gave the ratio in the reveal thread, correcting a reader's 4:1 estimate. The team has used the same cable (CBKnot 7/64 in Dyneema, about 1600 lb tensile strength) on all its string elevators since 2018. The elevator support plate and the sponsor panel protect the cable. It stretched slightly and was re-tensioned when loose; the team replaced it once during the season. The sideways layout let the stages rock front to back with the elevator up, which hurt L4 accuracy. The design lead measured the gap and cut new stage 0 top spreader plates with offset holes. Most of the loose-fit slop went away; the middle-stage tubes still deflect under drive acceleration. Swerve, per the SystemCore thread. That thread traces a fault on the back left module of the practice robot 'Omega' to a faulty MPM port for its CANcoder. The robot runs two CAN loops, one on the roboRIO and one on a CANivore. Module type: Unconfirmed (see CAD).

Why study it
PICK (CAD): rank 4 of 3690 on Local EPA (57-8), Regional Winners at Sacramento, Pinnacles and Contra Costa, with public Onshape CAD and a release page for code and scouting resources. Study the sideways elevator. The team states this layout rocks front to back with the elevator up, which hurt L4 accuracy. The design lead measured the slop and cut new stage 0 top spreader plates with offset holes; most of the loose-fit slop went away. The elevator runs on Dyneema cable at 3:1, the same cable the team has used on its string elevators since 2018. It stretched slightly and needed occasional re-tensioning. Lessons for students
  • Measure slop before you redesign: the team measured the front-back gap in its sideways elevator and fixed most of it with new spreader plates that have offset holes.
  • Cable-driven elevator: the team uses Dyneema cable as a lighter option for powering the elevator. Plan for re-tensioning, because the cable stretches slightly over time.
  • Bent parts: the team makes all bent aluminum parts from 5052 sheet, which it describes as more ductile than 6061, and reports no cracking.
  • Sensor layout: beam breaks on the coral end effector, the algae end effector and the indexer tell the code where each game piece is.
  • Two near-identical robots: the team 'leap-frogged' updates, so one robot was upgraded while the other ran driver practice and software. The practice robot 'Omega' later tested a new controller.

★ MadTown Robotics
Ground coral and algae intake with auto handoff; elevator on a pivot; differential wrist (pitch, roll); swerve; 3x Limelight 4

USA-CA · 53-3 · #5 · High confidence
Scoring (coral / algae)
A claw on a differential wrist scores coral and algae. The team runs the wrist in CTRE differential mode and lists its hurdles: fitting parts in the carriage, belt slip, and enough encoder wrap for a 1:1 reading. It used standard bevel gears, not spiral, and says it would try herringbone-style bevels next time. A retroreflective Banner sensor on a CANdi in the claw detects both pieces. The end effector motor runs in PWM mode to avoid a long CAN stub, after stator and current limits were set over CAN. A forum question names the motor as an X44; the team's answer does not restate the model.
Intake
Ground intake for coral and for algae. A diffuse Banner sensor on a CANdi sees the coral; the robot then lifts, hands off and enters a scoring state on its own. Ground algae hands off from the ground intake to the claw the same way. Reef algae pickup runs from a driver trigger: the robot squares up and follows a path to the ball, which lets the co-driver focus on lowering the coral intake.
Elevator / arm
The code models each position as a SuperstructureState with four values: elevator, elevator pivot, wrist pitch and wrist roll. A 'choreographer' compares current and target positions and builds a command that moves between them safely. Elevator tube is mainly .060 in wall for weight; each stage is about 21.25 in tall. Stage count: Unconfirmed (see the tech binder). Thinner 12/14 AWG wire and Alpha Wire let the team drop from a 15 x 30 mm to a 15 x 15 mm e-chain.
Drivetrain
Swerve, frame perimeter about 112 in per the team. A forum user read WCP X2 modules in two configurations from the tech binder; a team member stated the mix was for space and packaging. The A-frame tube support uses a 3/16 in aluminum plate with custom nut plates inside the 2x2 in box tube, plus a .090 in bent aluminum plate. The team uses the top of the swerve plate as structure, an idea it credits to 4414.
Endgame
Unconfirmed (not stated in the public release).
Auto
Autonomous Award at the Silicon Valley Regional. Autos start from a pre-defined path; Limelight object tracking builds a new path to the coral, which the team says happens 99.9% of the time. The camera tracks the ball and checks its height off the ground to confirm the coral is still there. The team has used the built-in Limelight model since 2023. Coral count: Unconfirmed (not stated in the public release).
Vision
Three Limelight 4 cameras. Two read AprilTags without the Hailo accelerator; the team saw better performance without it. The third runs object detection in auto only. Every camera has a milled heat sink; two of the three have fans.
Why study it
PICK (binder): rank 5 of 3690 on Local EPA (53-3), Championship Winner and Newton Division winner, with a public tech binder and a Q&A thread. Study the weight budget. The team states its practice robot weighed 130 lbs in week 5 and lists the steps it took: WCP SRPP, more pocketing, plastic and titanium bolts, drilled shafts, thinner plates and wire, and 3D-printed parts in place of metal. Also study how a 6 DOF robot is controlled: a differential wrist in CTRE differential mode, and code that names each position as a state of elevator, elevator pivot, wrist pitch and wrist roll. No CAD files are linked, so the binder and thread are the study material.
Lessons for students
  • Plan the weight budget early: the practice robot weighed 130 lbs in week 5, and the team lists each step it used to cut weight, down to 0.01-0.05 lb at a time.
  • Run the practice robot hard and print its parts in a weaker material: PETG HF parts fail earlier than the Markforged Onyx parts on the competition robot, and the competition robot sees only a few auto tunes and matches.
  • Wire Banner sensors through a CANdi: the team states detection was faster than with sensors plugged into the roboRIO.
  • Name superstructure positions as states: each state sets elevator, elevator pivot, wrist pitch and wrist roll, and one 'choreographer' plans the safe move between states.
  • Use object tracking to correct auto paths: the team says it would not grab the coral without Limelight tracking that rebuilds the path.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. A tech binder released by the team, plus team answers in the binder thread that name sensors, cameras, materials, wire sizes, the wrist drive mode and the code structure. No downloadable CAD. Released 2025-04-24 on Chief Delphi (tech binder).

Scoring (coral / algae)
End effector (manipulator) on the arm, with a roller reduction of about 11:1. Current sensing detects coral; the team tuned a voltage that stalls the roller and still grips the coral. The driver then presses a confirmation button to start the scoring sequence.
Intake
Ground coral intake. Wheels inside the intake center the coral, and the upper wheels spin it as it centers. One linebreak in the center detects coral; the intake then raises and hands off automatically. Two X60 motors drive it; the team says that is a lot of weight and plans X44s in future. The bottom roller has bearings but the team stopped it from spinning, which it says improves handoff and trough scoring. The intake zeros by current sensing.
Elevator / arm
Arm on a belt-driven elevator. The arm pivot has a 64.5:1 reduction. Software limits the arm to 290 deg left or right of straight down (580 deg range) and clamps the encoder range to 700 deg for safety. A WCP CAN throughbore encoder on a 2.22:1 reduction gives 800 deg of range and sets the arm position at match start; there are no hard stops. Arm wires pass through the pivot tube in wire wrap with slack. The elevator runs two open-end PolyBelt belts of about 13.5 ft, clamped to the carriage between aluminum and a profiled PLA part. Two motors with 9-tooth pinions drive 36-tooth gears, each on a shaft with a 24-tooth pulley. The carriage weighs about 7 lb, about 13.5 lb with the arm. The elevator zeros by current sensing. The team reports some elevator wobble and says the A-frame should have run to the corners.
Drivetrain
Drivetrain unconfirmed (see CAD).
Endgame
No climber. The team states the robot was at max weight, a climber would have needed many parts lightened, and it had about a week to build an untested climber. It used the time for LEDs and improvements to scoring, autos and the state machine.
Auto
The auto video shows the ground centering and handoff sequence. Coral count unconfirmed (see code).
Vision
Full-field pose estimation drives reef alignment. The team chose it over AprilTag tx/ty because the scoring point is offset from the robot center. Camera type and count unconfirmed.
Why study it
PICK (CAD + code): rank 17 of 3690 on Local EPA (53-12-0), Autonomous Award at Bonney Lake and Innovation in Control Award at Glacier Peak. Study the arm sensing. The arm can turn 290 deg either side of straight down, so one angle can match two physical positions. The team geared the absolute encoder at 2.22:1, a non-integer ratio, to give 800 deg of encoder range and give every position a distinct reading, and uses it only at match start. The arm has no hard stops; the elevator and intake zero by current sensing. The ground intake is the second lesson: inner wheels center the coral, one linebreak triggers raise and handoff, and the bottom roller is locked to make the handoff work.
Lessons for students
  • Pick an absolute encoder ratio above 1 and not a whole number when an arm turns more than one revolution: 2.22:1 gives 800 deg of encoder range for a 580 deg arm.

  • Use full-field pose, not tag tx/ty, when the scoring point is offset from the robot center.
  • Decide late additions by weight and time: at max weight with little room, the team skipped a climber and improved scoring and autos instead.

Intake
The Week 2 concept had a ground coral intake on the right side and a ground algae intake at the front, with coral station loading kept. The concept changed in Week 3; the team lists slightly narrower ground intakes as a con. Changed after Central Missouri: a floor coral intake inspired by 1690 and 118, plus a coral righting mechanism tested on 2025-03-12. At Greater Kansas City the intake chain bound; the team says the double sprockets did not seem to have enough spacing. The team removed the intake and used a feeder station funnel.
Elevator / arm
The Week 2 concept put the elevator at the back of the robot. Elevator based on the team's 2018/2019 design: 2x1x1/8 in C-channel stitch welded to 1.5 x 1/8 in flat bar, with delrin/acetal rollers on shoulder bolts. In Week 5 the team moved to side rigging with 4 spools and cascade rigging. The elevator calculation linked in the thread uses 2 Kraken X60 at a 5.333 reduction. The rebuild plan kept this elevator with fewer stages, because the arm reaches higher. At Greater Kansas City, 3/16 Dyneema could not lift the elevator; team 1802 lent 1/16 steel aircraft cable, and the V-groove bearings held it. Cable tension from the rigging angles stopped the elevator from going down much below its starting height.
Drivetrain
28 x 28 in frame. Swerve, per the team's posts: YAGSL configuration and 'snap angles' heading presets at 60 deg steps for the reef. The practice bot tested 3D printed 95A TPU wheels. On the competition robot the 3D printed wheels were slicks by the 2nd match of defense at Central Missouri. Module type unconfirmed (see CAD).

The Week 2 plan was a cage-shaped hole in the belly pan and a claw on one elevator stage. By Week 3 the team had a prototype climber claw that uses the elevator to lift. The climber hook was in CAD by Week 5. Climb at events, and deep or shallow cage, unconfirmed.
Auto
In Week 5 the team tested paths in AdvantageKit simulation for a 4-coral auto, all from the ground, ending with an algae run to the processor. The team calls these paths 'probably wishful thinking'. The retrospective says controls students had almost no time with the robot. Competition autos unconfirmed.
Vision
The team mentions a vision PC. Its Ethernet port burned out 2 days before Greater Kansas City; the team advises turning off PoE on the radio when it is not used. Cameras and software unconfirmed.
