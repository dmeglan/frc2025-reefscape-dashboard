★ OP Robotics · LIGHTNING
Riveted sheet-metal arm; counter-rotating roller coral intake; 2D AprilTag auto-align; swerve
123
Local EPA
Partial
Canada · 68-7 · #1
Scoring (coral / algae)
Scoring uses a 'dunk' step that the driver triggers after auto-align. The driver keeps this step so the robot can rescore after a miss and lose a second or two, not a full cycle.
Intake
Roller intake with top rollers and a bottom roller that turn in opposite directions; a small 1:1 gearbox reverses the bottom roller. The front roller floats, and coral compression changes with coral orientation. WCP stretch star wheels sit on 1.5 in OD, 1/8 in wall polycarbonate tube. Changed after practice from 1.25 in OD, 1/16 in wall tube after the tube bent in practice and the wheels migrated and slipped. The polycarbonate pivot is clamped with aluminum plates that spread bolt loads in impacts.
Elevator / arm
The arm is a riveted and formed 1/16 in aluminum structure. The robot uses 5052 aluminum sheet at 1/16 in and 1/8 in depending on load. The team drives pivots with large aluminum sprockets on a dead axle and chain: #25 for light-duty and #35 for heavy-duty pivots.
Drivetrain
Swerve with fully custom control and odometry, not CTRE's generated swerve code. Phoenix Pro features: FOC, fused CANcoder and time synchronization.
Endgame
Unconfirmed
Auto
Waypoints are hard-coded points, with no path-planning interface. The tolerance per waypoint ranges from 1.5 in to 48 in, depending on the action. In auto, the robot computes the coral position and generates a drive point with a heading that points at the coral. Auto selection inputs are read only after a 3 s timer in disabled, so the driver cannot change them between auto and teleop.
Vision
Limelight with 2D AprilTag tracking only; no MegaTag 1 or MegaTag 2. Distance comes from an interpolating tree map of tag area to measured distance. Auto-align drives to a pose relative to the reef tag for L2-L4 and uses global pose for L1. A drive-to-point controller runs two PID loops, on translation and rotation error, with an aggressive slew rate limiter.
Why study it
Study the auto-align, which uses only 2D Limelight tag data and no MegaTag pose. The drawing package keeps every part revision, marked R1, R2 and so on. Daly Division winner and FIRST Ontario Provincial Championship winner.
Lessons for students
  • Drawings can document a robot without a CAD model; follow the part revisions through the season.
  • The aluminum plates act as large washers; without them, side impacts can crack the polycarbonate at the bolt holes.
  • The team prefers chain to 5M HTD belt on pivots because chain is less likely to skip under shock loads, and designs in a chain tensioner, such as the inline turnbuckle tensioner used on the intake.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Team replies in the release thread add detail on auto-align, the swerve software, the intake rollers and sheet-metal materials. Released 2025-05-29 on Chief Delphi.

★ Jack in the Bot · Poi Pounder XXVI Spectre
Shoulder, extension and wrist arm; reef-AprilTag auto-align; latch climber with automated climb; swerve
113
Local EPA
Yes
USA-WA · 66-7 · #3
Scoring (coral / algae)
End effector on the arm wrist. It holds coral straight, as the intake delivers it, or horizontal in an L1-optimized position, and it also handles algae. Scoring uses one button for a 'base' setpoint and one for a 'top' setpoint. Translation, rotation, release, wait and backout are all automated.
Intake
The team never used coral station intaking during teleop. On station loading, a team reply says it is better to drive firmly into the station and guarantee the load than to risk wheel slip leaving the robot short.
Elevator / arm
Arm with a shoulder pivot, an extension and a wrist. The code treats each as its own subsystem, which the team says gives cleaner compartmentalization. Shoulder and wrist are commanded by angle. Positions come from the built-in Kraken encoders on the shoulder, extension and wrist. A re-homing button was used at most twice in the season, after heavy arm impacts; it mainly saves time in driver practice. The arm code ran on the team's 2023 robot before bring-up.
Drivetrain
Swerve. A skew compensation constant corrects drift off center when the robot rotates while translating. The code uses voltage targets, not duty cycle, so output holds as battery voltage drops; the reply does not say which motors.
Endgame
Climber with latches and climber intake wheels. The climb is fully automated once the bar is in the latches. The code detects the bar from the speed and current of the climber intake wheel motor, and the team reports no false positives.
Auto
Autonomous Award at PNW District Auburn, Sammamish and the PNW DCMP. The team mentions a 'mark auto' and tested code to knock algae off the mark; that code was not used.
Vision
Cameras read reef AprilTags. There is no pose estimator: the code hard-resets swerve odometry to camera poses, and only while auto-aligning, when the robot is stable. The team reports vision as accurate at the scoring position, with more error farther out. Drivers switch between pose-based and rotation-based reef selection; LEDs show purple for pose-based and yellow for rotation-based.
Why study it
Study the driver automation the team explains in its threads: PID auto-align, one-button scoring and an automated climb. Read the code thread with the repository open. Championship winner.
Lessons for students
  • A plain PID auto-align with a velocity cap and friction compensation can be enough: the team tried trapezoidal profiles and dynamic path following and kept PID.
  • Relocalize only when the data is good, not during fast traversals.
  • Give drivers a fallback when odometry drifts.
  • Motor data can replace a sensor, as the climber shows.
  • Bind buttons to robot state: one button does four things depending on the piece held (straight coral, horizontal coral, algae, none). Drivers use Xbox controllers with back paddles.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Team replies in the reveal and code threads explain the auto-align control, vision relocalization, automated scoring, controller bindings and arm sensing. Released 2025-04-23 on Chief Delphi.

Scoring (coral / algae)
The team's SystemCore thread lists separate beam breaks for a coral end effector and an algae end effector. All scoring runs on one controller through back paddles, with an autoscore function.
Intake
The SystemCore thread lists an indexer beam break.
Elevator / arm
Sideways elevator driven by Dyneema cable at a 3:1 reduction; the OP gave the ratio in the reveal thread, correcting a reader's 4:1 estimate. The team has used the same cable (CBKnot 7/64 in Dyneema, about 1600 lb tensile strength) on all its string elevators since 2018. The elevator support plate and the sponsor panel protect the cable. It stretched slightly and was re-tensioned when loose; the team replaced it once during the season. The sideways layout let the stages rock front to back with the elevator up, which hurt L4 accuracy. The design lead measured the gap and cut new stage 0 top spreader plates with offset holes. Most of the loose-fit slop went away; the middle-stage tubes still deflect under drive acceleration.
Drivetrain
Swerve. The SystemCore thread traces a fault on the back left module of the practice robot 'Omega' to a faulty MPM port for its CANcoder. The robot runs two CAN loops, one on the roboRIO and one on a CANivore.

★ MadTown Robotics
Claw on a differential wrist; ground coral and algae intake with auto handoff; elevator on a pivot; swerve
106
Local EPA
Partial
USA-CA · 53-3 · #5
Scoring (coral / algae)
A claw on a differential wrist scores coral and algae. The team runs the wrist in CTRE differential mode and lists its hurdles: fitting parts in the carriage, belt slip, and enough encoder wrap for a 1:1 reading. It used standard bevel gears, not spiral, and says it would try herringbone-style bevels next time. A retroreflective Banner sensor on a CANdi in the claw detects both pieces. The end effector motor runs in PWM mode to avoid a long CAN stub, after stator and current limits were set over CAN. A forum question names the motor as an X44; the team's answer does not restate the model.
Intake
Ground intake for coral and for algae. A diffuse Banner sensor on a CANdi sees the coral; the robot then lifts, hands off and enters a scoring state on its own. Ground algae hands off from the ground intake to the claw the same way. Reef algae pickup runs from a driver trigger: the robot squares up and follows a path to the ball, which lets the co-driver focus on lowering the coral intake.
Elevator / arm
The code models each position as a SuperstructureState with four values: elevator, elevator pivot, wrist pitch and wrist roll. A 'choreographer' compares current and target positions and builds a command that moves between them safely. Elevator tube is mainly .060 in wall for weight; each stage is about 21.25 in tall. Thinner 12/14 AWG wire and Alpha Wire let the team drop from a 15 x 30 mm to a 15 x 15 mm e-chain.
Drivetrain
Swerve, frame perimeter about 112 in. A forum user read WCP X2 modules in two configurations from the tech binder; a team member stated the mix was for space and packaging. The A-frame tube support uses a 3/16 in aluminum plate with custom nut plates inside the 2x2 in box tube, plus a .090 in bent aluminum plate. The team uses the top of the swerve plate as structure, an idea it credits to 4414.
Endgame
Unconfirmed
Auto
Autonomous Award at the Silicon Valley Regional. Autos start from a pre-defined path; Limelight object tracking builds a new path to the coral, which the team says happens 99.9% of the time. The camera tracks the ball and checks its height off the ground to confirm the coral is still there. The team has used the built-in Limelight model since 2023.
Vision
Three Limelight 4 cameras. Two read AprilTags without the Hailo accelerator; the team saw better performance without it. The third runs object detection in auto only. Every camera has a milled heat sink; two of the three have fans.
Why study it
Study the weight budget: the practice robot weighed 130 lbs in week 5, and the team lists each step it took to cut weight. Also study how a 6 DOF robot is controlled: a differential wrist in CTRE differential mode, and code that names each position as a state. Championship Winner and Newton Division winner.
Lessons for students
  • Plan the weight budget early. The team cut weight with WCP SRPP, more pocketing, plastic and titanium bolts, drilled shafts, thinner plates and wire, and 3D-printed parts in place of metal, down to 0.01-0.05 lb at a time.
  • Run the practice robot hard and print its parts in a weaker material: PETG HF parts fail earlier than the Markforged Onyx parts on the competition robot, which sees only a few auto tunes and matches.
  • Wire Banner sensors through a CANdi: the team states detection was faster than with sensors plugged into the roboRIO.
  • Use object tracking to correct auto paths: the team says it would not grab the coral without it.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Team answers in the binder thread name sensors, cameras, materials, wire sizes, the wrist drive mode and the code structure. Released 2025-04-24 on Chief Delphi (tech binder).

★ Chill Out · SubZero
580 deg arm on a belt elevator; ground coral intake with auto handoff; pose-based auto-align; no climber
93
Local EPA
Yes
USA-WA · 53-12 · #17
Scoring (coral / algae)
End effector on the arm, with a roller reduction of about 11:1. Current sensing detects coral; the team tuned a voltage that stalls the roller and still grips the coral. To score, the arm moves down with the elevator while the bumpers rest against the reef. Scoring is semi-automatic: when the driver releases the sticks, the robot drives to the nearest scoring position and picks the closer branch. The driver then presses a confirmation button to start the scoring sequence.
Intake
Ground coral intake. Wheels inside the intake center the coral, and the upper wheels spin it as it centers. One linebreak in the center detects coral; the intake then raises and hands off automatically. Two X60 motors drive it; the team says that is a lot of weight and plans X44s in future. The bottom roller has bearings but the team stopped it from spinning, which it says improves handoff and trough scoring. The intake zeros by current sensing.
Elevator / arm
Arm on a belt-driven elevator. The arm pivot has a 64.5:1 reduction. Software limits the arm to 290 deg left or right of straight down (580 deg range) and clamps the encoder range to 700 deg for safety. A WCP CAN throughbore encoder on a 2.22:1 reduction gives 800 deg of range and sets the arm position at match start; there are no hard stops. Arm wires pass through the pivot tube in wire wrap with slack. The elevator runs two open-end PolyBelt belts of about 13.5 ft, clamped to the carriage between aluminum and a profiled PLA part. Two motors with 9-tooth pinions drive 36-tooth gears, each on a shaft with a 24-tooth pulley. The carriage weighs about 7 lb, about 13.5 lb with the arm. The elevator zeros by current sensing. The team reports some elevator wobble and says the A-frame should have run to the corners.
Drivetrain
Unconfirmed
Endgame
No climber. The robot was at max weight, a climber would have needed many parts lightened, and the team had about a week to build an untested climber. It used the time for LEDs and improvements to scoring, autos and the state machine.
Auto
The auto video shows the ground centering and handoff sequence.
Vision
Full-field pose estimation drives reef alignment.
Why study it
Study the arm sensing: a non-integer absolute encoder ratio gives every arm position a distinct reading, with no hard stops. The ground intake is the second lesson: one linebreak triggers raise and handoff. Autonomous Award at Bonney Lake and Innovation in Control Award at Glacier Peak.
Lessons for students
  • Pick an absolute encoder ratio above 1 and not a whole number when an arm turns more than one revolution.
  • One centered linebreak can trigger raise and handoff only if the coral is already centered by the time the intake is up.
  • Use full-field pose, not tag tx/ty, when the scoring point is offset from the robot center.
  • Decide late additions by weight and time, as the team did with the climber.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Team replies in the release thread state the intake handoff logic, the arm encoder ratio and reductions, the elevator gearing and the auto-align control scheme. Released 2025-05-09 on Chief Delphi.
